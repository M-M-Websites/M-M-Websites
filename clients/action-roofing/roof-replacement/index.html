Signs it may be time for a new roof

If any of these sound familiar, it’s worth a call.

  • Curling, cracked or bald shingles across the roof
  • Leaks in different spots, again and again
  • Sagging along the roofline
  • Repairs that don’t hold for long
  • Damage across large sections after a storm
  • Daylight or damp boards in the attic

What goes into a new shingle roof

Scroll to see a shingle roof go on, from the bare deck to the gutters.

  1. 01Roof deckThe plywood or plank base that everything else is fastened to.
  2. 02Drip edgeMetal edging along the eaves and rakes that carries water off the edge and into the gutter.
  3. 03UnderlaymentA water-resistant layer rolled out over the deck: a second line of defense under the shingles.
  4. 04Starter stripA first row along the eave that seals the bottom edge so wind can’t get under it.
  5. 05ShinglesLaid in overlapping courses from the eave up, each row covering the nails of the row below.
  6. 06Ridge capCaps folded over the peak, where the two slopes meet.
  7. 07GuttersCatch the water at the eave and carry it away from the walls and foundation.

Every roof is different. Ask what yours needs when you request an estimate.
